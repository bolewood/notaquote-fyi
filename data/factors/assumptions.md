# Factor engine: builder's notes

Checked 22 September 2026. The plain-language guide is `README.md` in this folder; this file records the engineering choices behind it.

- One engine. `estimate()` in `src/lib/factor-engine.ts` turns a starting premium (yours, or a typical one) plus the scenario it belongs to into an estimate for another scenario, using index(new) ÷ index(start). `compareVehicles()` runs one driver across many cars; `whatIf()` compares two scenarios and writes the headline. `runFactorEngine()` is the older calculator entry point and now calls `estimate()`.
- Exact arithmetic. Factors are whole hundredths. Products are BigInt fractions. Dollars round half up once, at the end. The range combines basis-point spreads in quadrature with an integer square root, so every result is reproducible.
- The index is `whole × (liability share × limits × vehicle liability + damage share × deductible × loan or lease × vehicle age × vehicle damage)`. The damage share is zero without collision and comprehensive.
- The range. With your own premium and no change, the estimate is your premium, and the range is only the $1 needed to keep low < likely < high. Each changed factor adds its spread (both its old and new value), weighted by the share of the premium it touches. A typical starting price adds the company-to-company spread from the surveys. A partly matched trim, a class-average or unknown vehicle, a model year outside HLDI's years, and a state change without typical prices each add their own spread.
- No double counting. The teen switch is ignored: the 16–18 age band is the teen factor. Years licensed only counts from age 26 (California, our source for it, rates by years licensed instead of age, so it partly stands in for age). Good student counts under 26, driver training under 22. Deductible, loan or lease, and vehicle age only count with collision and comprehensive.
- Credit is not modeled and has no control.
- State. With your own premium, a state change uses the ratio of typical state premiums when the caller passes them (`stateAnnual`); otherwise the likely figure stays and the range widens a lot. The typical-premium file is built separately (Workstream 2, state starting point).
- Guardrails. `assertFactorBundleSafe()` checks that every cell has a basis, that sourced and rough cells name a listed source and say how they were derived, that estimates say so ("estimate" and "help wanted") and widen the range, that reference cells are exactly 1.00, and that there is no credit factor. `factor-derivation.test.ts` re-runs the derivation and requires the committed JSON to match.
- The trend (BLS insurance CPI) is still not applied.
