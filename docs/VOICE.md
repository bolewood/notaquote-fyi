# How NotAQuote.FYI talks

Write like a friendly, well-informed parent explaining car insurance at the kitchen table. They know the subject, they're honest about what they don't know, and they want you to leave the table feeling smarter and less anxious. They are not a lawyer, an actuary, or a salesperson.

## The rules

1. **Plain words first.** If a 15-year-old wouldn't know the word, use a simpler one or explain it in the same sentence. "Deductible" gets a short explanation the first time it appears. "Relativity", "anchor", "baseline", "thin", "lawful sensitivity factor", "not cleared", "for counsel" never appear in the interface.
2. **Say it once.** The "this is not a quote" message appears once per page, in one clear place. It doesn't go in every panel, and every panel doesn't need to repeat it.
3. **Be honest in human terms.** Don't say "Confidence: low. Baseline not cleared." Say "This is a ballpark, not a price. Real quotes can land well above or below it." Uncertainty should sound helpful, not defensive.
4. **Show your work, invitingly.** Every number links to where it came from. Say "Here's how we got this", not "Methodology". Say "Spot something wrong? Tell us", not "Corrections queue".
5. **Short sentences, active voice, second person.** "You pay", "your car", "we estimate".
6. **Warm, not cute.** No exclamation-point pileups, no puns in the middle of a number, no fake enthusiasm. One light touch per page at most.
7. **Numbers are friendly.** Use "$1,850 a year (about $155 a month)" and round sensibly. Ranges read as "roughly $1,500–$2,300".
8. **Never scare, never sell.** Don't push anyone toward more or less coverage. Explain the trade-off and let them decide.

## Legal honesty without legalese

We still say, clearly and once: we're not an insurer or agent, this isn't a quote, and real prices come from insurers. We still cite every source. Precision about what we know is the trust, not the length of the disclaimer.
