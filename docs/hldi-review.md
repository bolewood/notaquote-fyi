# NotAQuote.FYI: changes after HLDI's review

November 5, 2026

Thank you for reviewing NotAQuote.FYI. You asked that the site either match HLDI's published results or plainly explain why it looks different. This note shows what we changed, using the 2024 Subaru Legacy as the example, and lists what we still need from you.

NotAQuote.FYI is a non-commercial planning tool that estimates what a car, a teen driver, or a move would do to a family's car insurance bill. It isn't an insurance company and doesn't sell anything.

## What changed, in short

- **HLDI's numbers come first.** Every car page now shows what HLDI reports, coverage by coverage, in HLDI's own terms, before showing our price estimate.
- **We explain the gap.** Right after HLDI's numbers, one plain paragraph explains why our price difference is smaller than HLDI's loss difference, worked out from the same numbers our estimate uses.
- **We use HLDI's words.** "Claims data" and "claims results" are now "loss data" or "insurance loss results" across the site. The ratings use HLDI's five levels.
- **We say what's our choice.** The site says plainly that HLDI publishes each coverage separately, and that combining them and counting only part of the result is our own choice, made to estimate a price.
- **We no longer say "because insurers do the same."** It now says a car's loss record is only one of many things insurers weigh, and how each weighs it is a trade secret.

None of this changed any price on the site. It changed only the wording, the labels, and the order.

## The example: the 2024 Subaru Legacy

This is the text a visitor now reads on the Subaru Legacy page and on our "How the math works" page. The site writes it from the same numbers it uses for the price, so nothing below is typed in by hand.

### Step 1: What HLDI reports (2022–24 models, all drivers combined)

> The Highway Loss Data Institute (HLDI) compares each model's insurance losses with an average car's. For the Subaru Legacy 4WD with EyeSight, on HLDI's scale where 100 is an average car and lower is better, it reports 79 for crash repairs, 96 for non-crash damage, 82 for damage to others' property, and 76 for injury claims.

| Coverage | HLDI's rating | Compared with an average car |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crash repairs (collision) | Better than average | 21% better than average |
| Non-crash damage (comprehensive) | Average | 4% better than average |
| Damage to others' property (property damage liability) | Average | 18% better than average |
| Injuries to others (bodily injury liability) | Better than average | injury claims filed 24% less often than average |

Each percentage is 100 minus HLDI's index (for example, 79 becomes "21% better than average"). The page links to HLDI's table and names the report window.

### Step 2: What we estimate that means for price

> Prices move less than loss results do: a car's loss record is only one of many things insurers weigh, and each weighs it differently. So we count only part of HLDI's difference. Combining crash repairs and non-crash damage (weighted about 71% and 29%, the share each makes up of industry claim costs), the Legacy comes out about 16% better than average. We count about 40% of that difference, so the part of the bill that fixes the car is about 6% less than average: about $900 a year vs $950. On the liability side, damage to others and injury claims combine to about 21% better than average. We count about half, so that part is about 11% less: $880 a year vs $990.

The 71% and 29% weights are collision's and comprehensive's shares of industry claim costs per insured car in 2024 (ISO figures, as published by the Insurance Information Institute). The liability side uses the same source: about 43% property damage and 57% bodily injury.

For cars where HLDI doesn't publish one of the coverages, the paragraph says so instead of describing a combination. For luxury makes, where our estimate also adds what the car costs to replace, it says HLDI's results don't measure that.

### The note about drivers

Next to the example driver on every car page:

> HLDI's results combine drivers of all ages. We apply them to one example driver (a 45-year-old with a clean record) so pages are comparable; they aren't specific to 45-year-olds.

### Medical payments and PIP

We don't show medical payments or personal injury protection results on car pages. The "How the math works" page says:

> HLDI also publishes medical payments and personal injury protection (PIP) results. We don't use them, because med pay covers people in your own car and PIP rules vary by state.

## Coverage names we use everywhere

| HLDI coverage | Our name | Helper text shown with it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Collision | Crash repairs (collision) | fixing this car after a crash |
| Comprehensive | Non-crash damage (comprehensive) | theft, weather, fire, vandalism, glass, and animal strikes |
| Property damage liability | Damage to others' property (property damage liability) | other people's cars and property, when this car's driver is at fault |
| Bodily injury liability | Injuries to others (bodily injury liability) | how often injury claims are filed against this car's drivers, not how much they cost |

When we explain why one car costs more or less than another, we use the same coverages, for example "lower crash-repair losses, less damage to others' property, and fewer injury claims". That replaces the old "fewer at-fault claims".

## The rating levels we use (provisional)

We use HLDI's five rating levels. Until you confirm the cutoffs, we use these, on HLDI's index where 100 is an average car:

| HLDI's index | Rating |
| --- | --- |
| 50 or less | Substantially better than average |
| 51 to 79 | Better than average |
| 80 to 122 | Average |
| 123 to 149 | Worse than average |
| 150 or more | Substantially worse than average |

The cutoffs live in one place on the site, so changing them is quick.

## What we need from you

1. **Please confirm the rating cutoffs** above, or send the ones HLDI uses.
2. **Please send your revised wording for our HLDI paragraph.** On the "How the math works" page, the paragraph describing what HLDI publishes sits in its own marked box, ready to be replaced word for word. Today it reads:

   > HLDI publishes how each model's insurance losses compare with an average car's, for each coverage separately: overall losses (how often claims happen times how much they cost) for crash repairs (collision), non-crash damage (comprehensive: theft, weather, fire, vandalism, glass, and animal strikes), and damage to others' property (property damage liability), and how often injury claims are filed for injuries to others (bodily injury liability). HLDI's results combine drivers of all ages. For each car's model year, we use the most recent HLDI report whose three-year window includes that year (for example, the 2020–22 report for a 2020 car, or 2022–24 for a 2023 car), and use the nearest report only when no window covers the year.

3. **Anything else that reads wrong.** If any sentence above misdescribes HLDI's data, tell us and we'll fix it.

## Keeping it current

HLDI publishes new results every January. We've added a January checklist to our data-refresh guide: fetch the new window, rebuild, update the dates, and check the car pages against your published table. Our sources page shows "HLDI data through 2022–24 models, retrieved October 2026".
